In force 1 July 2024

Section 302 is now Section 103.

On 1 July 2024 India replaced its three foundational criminal codes. Every section was renumbered, some were merged, a few were split, and a handful were dropped altogether. Ask a chatbot what a provision became and it will often answer confidently and incompletely. This assistant answers from the official gazette and the government’s own correspondence tables, and shows you the source for every claim.

Ask it something

A working assistant, running in this page.

Name a provision — “IPC 302”, “BNSS Section 173”, “Section 65B of the Indian Evidence Act” — or describe an offence. Answers are composed from the official correspondence tables and the gazette text, in your browser, with the source for every claim.

Why not just ask ChatGPT?

We did. The same 44 questions — held out of training from the start — were put to ChatGPT on 19 September 2026 with no retrieval, and scored by identical code. It got 25 right; this system got 27. That is not the interesting part. The two fail in opposite places.

Scored on whether the answer names every provision the correct answer names. ChatGPT knows what provisions are about, so it handles a section number reused for a different offence. It does not have the correspondence table, so when one new provision absorbed four old ones it names the first and stops.

How it answers

Four steps, and the third one is the point.

Read the question for citations

“BNSS Section 482” and “CrPC Section 482” are parsed out of the question directly, in every form they are written — “IPC 302”, “Section 65B of the Indian Evidence Act, 1872”, “Sections 415, 417, 418, 419 and 420”.

Search 2,819 passages

One passage per section, per First Schedule entry and per judgment, embedded with bge-small-en-v1.5 and searched with FAISS. Exact statutory citations are matched outright and ranked above similarity hits, which took recall at three passages from 26% to 96%.

Look the counterparts up deliberately

Similarity search will never reliably surface the counterpart of a provision: nothing about the words of IPC 302 resembles the words of BNS 103 more than any other homicide section. So counterparts come from the concordance instead. This single change took the share of hard questions whose context held every provision needed from 34% to 98%.

Answer, cite, and refuse when out of scope

Every answer carries the provisions it rests on and a link to the gazette PDF or reported judgment. Requests for advice, for loopholes, or to act as an advocate are refused before the model ever sees them.

Scope

What it will not do.

This is an informational tool, not a lawyer. These are refused in code, not merely discouraged by training — the question is checked against a pattern list before it reaches the model, so a refusal cannot be talked out of it.

Run it yourself

It works with no model loaded at all.

In sources-only mode the assistant returns the retrieved law itself rather than composing prose — which, given the limitation below, is the mode to trust. 

# the retriever and concordance, no model required
python scripts/bot.py --sources-only "Which BNS section replaced IPC Section 302?"

# with the fine-tuned model, if you have one saved
python scripts/bot.py --model-dir path/to/flan-t5-small-context "Is theft bailable?"

# rebuild every dataset from the official sources
python scripts/scrape_acts.py && python scripts/build_mapping_table.py
python scripts/validate_data.py   # expects 0 failures

Honest limitations

What is still wrong with it.

The retrieval layer and the concordance are verified against the gazette and pass every validation check. The fine-tuned language model on top of them is a different matter, and reading all 44 of its answers — rather than only its scores — turned up three defects.

01

Section numbers drift to neighbours

Asked about BNSS 482 the model discusses BNSS 481; asked about BSA 114 it discusses BSA 115. All nine of its failures on same-number questions do this, with both provisions sitting in its context. A 77-million-parameter model copying a three-digit number.

02

It corrupts statutory titles

Including in answers scored correct: BNS 324 (Mizachief), BNS 303 (Trash) for theft. The citation is right and the prose is not.

03

It invents counterparts for repealed provisions

Ten of ten times. Asked what replaced IPC 124A it answers “BNS Section 132 (Sedition)”, when sedition was not carried forward at all. The citation metric scored this 100% because the correct answer cites only the old section — a reminder to read outputs, not only scores.

04

So every answer on this page is assembled, not generated

The answers above are composed from the concordance and the gazette text in the retrieval index, which are verified. None is the fine-tuned model’s prose. The two examples of model output on this page are labelled as such.

This is not legal advice.

It describes what the law and reported decisions say, and cites its source. It does not advise on any specific matter, does not suggest ways to evade liability, and does not represent anyone.
